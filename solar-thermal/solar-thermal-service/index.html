Solar thermal service

A solar thermal system heats water from the sun. It is not solar PV. Servicing it regularly — often once a year — keeps the collectors, glycol circuit and controls working. The list below is what a standard visit usually covers where the plant is accessible.

This is the hub’s annual checklist, written as a homeowner’s guide. It is not a method statement and not a price. Roof work, pressurised glycol and unvented cylinders need a competent person. If the original install was MCS, use an MCS solar thermal installer for warranty-sensitive work.

Panels

  • Visual inspection of the evacuated tubes or the flat-plate collector
  • Visual inspection of the fixings
  • Clean the collectors if they need it

How the two collector types sit on a roof is on solar thermal.

System

  • Operate the air-removal devices
  • Set pump speed and flow rate
  • Check expansion-vessel pressure
  • Check system pressure
  • Operate the pressure-relief valves
  • Check for leaks

Thermal fluid

  • Check glycol (antifreeze) concentration
  • Check glycol pH
  • Replace the glycol when it is spent

If the glycol has to come out because the system has overheated (stagnation), the hub practice is to powerflush first, then refill. Heat-damaged fluid leaves residue; pouring new glycol on top of that is a short service. If the glycol is only old, drain and refill with new antifreeze.

Controller

  • Check the controller settings
  • Record hours-run
  • Investigate if the system has overheated
  • Explain the solar controls to the householder, including how to top up the store when the sun has not

A solar store can still feed a combi through an Intasol combi diverter valve. That does not remove the need to service the solar circuit.

Do not treat this as a DIY drain-down. Solar loops run hotter than a heating circuit, the fluid is not central-heating inhibitor, and a roof collector can stagnate at well above boiling if the pump is off on a clear day. Leave the glycol and the relief valves to someone who has done this plant before.
