Boiler Repairs

A boiler repair is work to get heat or hot water back after a fault — not the same as an annual service. Any work on a gas appliance in the UK must be done by a Gas Safe registered engineer. If you can smell gas, hear a leak, or a carbon monoxide alarm is sounding, leave the property and call the National Gas Emergency Service on 0800 111 999.

What you can check first

These checks do not involve opening the gas appliance. If any of them restore heat, you may not need a call-out.

  • Confirm the programmer or thermostat is calling for heat, and that TRVs on radiators are open.
  • Look at the boiler display. A lockout or fault code is useful for the engineer — see our fault codes A–Z.
  • Check system pressure on a sealed system. Many combis want about 1–1.5 bar when cold. Filling loops are usually on the underside of the boiler; if you are not sure, leave it. Guide: boiler pressure.
  • Use the manufacturer reset only as the instructions describe. Repeated resets without a repair can mask a real fault.
  • Frozen condensate pipes are common in cold weather. Thawing from the outside with warm water is a homeowner job; do not use boiling water on plastic pipe.

When to call an engineer

Call a Gas Safe engineer if there is no heat or hot water after those checks, a persistent fault code, a leak from the boiler, ignition failure, or you are not confident topping up pressure. Same-day attendance matters most in winter, for vulnerable occupants, or where a leak is causing damage.

A repair is not a substitute for a service. A well installed, set-up, and serviced boiler fails less often. If the appliance is old, beyond economical repair, or still under manufacturer warranty, say so when you book — warranty work often has to go through the maker, not a third-party call-out.
