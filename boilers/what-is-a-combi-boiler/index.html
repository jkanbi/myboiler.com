What is a combi boiler?

A combination boiler is one appliance that does central heating and instantaneous hot water. No hot-water cylinder, no loft cold-water or feed-and-expansion tanks — unless something else on site needs them.

A combi (combination boiler) packs space heating and domestic hot water into one case. Open a hot tap and cold mains water is heated on demand through a plate heat exchanger (or a combined heat exchanger on some designs). The same burner heats the radiators when the heating calls. That is why combis took over UK flats and many houses: one cupboard, no cylinder waiting to run out of store.

It is still a sealed heating system. Pressure, inhibitor and a filling loop matter the same way they do on a system boiler. The difference is that hot water does not come from a cylinder coil — it is made as you use it. Side-by-side with the other types: boiler types.

Advantages

  • Continuous hot water while the boiler and the cold main are working — there is no cylinder store to empty.
  • Space saving — no hot-water cylinder and usually no loft tanks.
  • Mains-pressure taps — shower pressure follows the cold main. You do not need a cold-water storage cistern or a booster pump unless the private supply is weak.
  • Simpler install than a full cylinder system when the pipework and flue path allow it — fewer vessels and less copper in the loft.

Disadvantages

  • No immersion backup. If the boiler fails, you lose heating and hot water together. An electric shower on the cold main is the usual partial fallback.
  • More DHW parts. Diverting valves, plate heat exchangers and inlet filters are common wear and scale points. Some boilers (for example Intergas combined-HEX designs) remove the separate plate; that is not true of every make.
  • Flow rate is limited by the boiler’s DHW output and by the incoming cold main. One decent shower or bath at a time is the honest expectation. Storage combis with a small built-in store help the first draw; they do not invent two simultaneous baths from a weak main.
  • Renewables need a store. A standard combi does not take solar thermal or a heat pump as a simple bolt-on the way a cylinder system can. Hybrid and diverter products exist (see solar thermal and the Intasol exception) — they are not “combi alone plus panels”.

A combi does not raise mains pressure. If the kitchen cold tap is weak, the hot tap will be weak. Measure flow before you buy a bigger badge. See low mains water pressure.

When a combi is the right answer

  • Limited space and no room (or appetite) for a cylinder.
  • One bathroom, or simultaneous demand that a measured main and a correctly sized combi can actually meet.
  • A cold main with enough pressure and flow — typically what flats and many modern houses already have.

Large homes, two baths at once, or a weak private supply usually want a system or regular boiler with a sized cylinder — not a louder combi. Type choice in a real house sits on choosing a new boiler; sizing is on what size boiler.
