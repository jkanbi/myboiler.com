BUS grant scheme for installers

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme is paid to the MCS installer, not the householder. You apply on the owner’s behalf and take the grant off the quote and the invoice. This page is the path to being allowed to do that.

Guidance only, checked against public Ofgem, MCS and RECC pages in October 2026. Fees, lead times and certification bodies change. This is not an application service and not legal advice. Start from Ofgem’s installer page and the live BUS installer guidance.

England and Wales only. Scotland and Northern Ireland run separate schemes. Hybrids that keep a fossil-fuel boiler are not eligible. You must pass the full grant through as an upfront discount — Ofgem can reject or revoke a voucher if you do not.

Current grant values

The installer claims these and deducts them on the quote. Figures below follow the GOV.UK notice effective from 21 July 2026. Trust the live tables if they have moved.

Installation Grant
Air-to-water or ground source heat pump £7,500
Air-to-water or ground source, off-gas grid, replacing oil or LPG (until 31 March 2027) £9,000
Air-to-air heat pump (residential only) £2,500
Biomass boiler (rural, never on gas grid; extra rules) £5,000

What you need before you can offer BUS

Do these in roughly this order. Consumer-code applications still take weeks if you need one, so start that early.

1. MCS-approved training

You need competence for both installation and design of the technology you will certify (heat pumps and/or biomass). MCS lists approved courses. A Technical Supervisor must be able to sign off each job against the MCS installation standard.

2. Public liability insurance

Certification bodies typically want a minimum of £2 million public liability. Trade insurers (Admiral Business, Trade Direct and others) sell this; premiums vary with turnover and claims. The old hub “from about £600” figure is only a ballpark — get a quote.

3. Consumer code

Ofgem still expects an approved consumer code on BUS work. As of the installer guidance we read:

  • If you are on the MCS Redeveloped Installer Scheme (MCS:2025), that scheme itself is an approved consumer code for BUS. You do not also need RECC or HIES for the grant.
  • If you are still on the previous MCS scheme, you must hold RECC or HIES. Start this first — the hub’s 2–4 week lead time is still a fair warning.

RECC fees on their public table (exclusive of VAT, checked October 2026): joining / administration fee £100; sole trader membership £226; 1–6 employees £299. The hub’s £215 sole-trader joining figure is out of date. Other company types: RECC fees. Ask HIES for their current schedule.

4. Quality management system (QMS)

You need documented processes for MCS before the certification-body assessment. Options the hub listed still exist: a packaged QMS (EasyMCS, Paperclip and similar), or your own system using MCS templates. We could not verify the old EasyMCS “one technology £625 / second £100” prices on their public site — ask the supplier. The QMS has to exist for the assessment; some installers put it in place before they buy public liability.

5. Apply to a certification body

The certification body (CB) assesses your office processes and at least one compliant installation per technology. MCS lists current CBs, including NAPIT, NICEIC, OFTEC, Simply Certification and others. The hub named APHC; MCS stated APHC’s UKAS accreditation was suspended when we checked, so they could not make certification decisions. Use the live MCS becoming-certified list. CBs also want that £2 million public liability.

6. MCS registration

Once the CB is satisfied you sign the MCS installer agreement and get access to the MCS Installations Database. MCS says the company listing itself is then quick. Ongoing costs include a per-certificate fee (MCS published £30 per registered installation). Pathway: mcscertified.com/installers/becoming-certified.

7. Create a BUS installer account

Open the account on the Ofgem BUS installer pages before you need to submit a voucher. The hub’s “21 days activation” mixed two facts: you can apply once the account is live; MCS’s Find an Installer filter for BUS-ready firms can take up to 21 days after account activation to show your name.

How a BUS job then runs

  1. Check the property and technology against the live Ofgem rules (England or Wales, replace fossil or electric heat, EPC or alternative evidence, no fossil hybrid).
  2. Quote with the full grant shown as a deduction. Do not ask the owner to pay that slice.
  3. Apply for the voucher on the owner’s behalf. Ofgem emails them for consent.
  4. Install and commission within the voucher window (typically three months; six months for ground source). Then redeem against the MCS certificate.

Ofgem can audit after payment. Keep the MCS certificate, quote, invoice and consent trail.
