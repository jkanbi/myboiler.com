Intasol Combi Diverter Valve

Links a solar-heated unvented cylinder to a combi boiler, so stored solar water goes to the taps when it is hot enough, and into the combi as a pre-heat when it is not.

What it does

The valve sits between a solar-heated unvented hot water cylinder and a combi boiler. If the cylinder drops below a usable temperature, it diverts that water to the combi and treats it as incoming pre-heat. It then blends water from the combi, or from the cylinder, with mains cold so the outlet is a safe temperature for the taps.

The Hub review of this setup puts the changeover at 48°C. At 48°C or above, cylinder water goes straight to the taps at a set temperature. Below 48°C, the water is sent to the combi at about 28°C and brought up to the combi’s hot-water setpoint, instead of the combi heating from cold. Either path then goes through a thermostatic mixing valve before the taps.

Features

  • Links a solar-heated unvented cylinder to a combi boiler
  • WRAS approved
  • Diverts to the combi when the cylinder temperature is low
  • Uses the solar-heated water in the cylinder first
  • Blends the outlet to a usable hot-water temperature
  • Maximum static pressure 10 bar
  • Maximum dynamic pressure 5 bar
  • Temperature adjustment 35–55°C, stability ±2°C
  • Maximum inlet temperature 110°C
  • Minimum flow for stable operation 6 l/min

The same Hub note put the valve at about £300. That figure is from 2018, so check a current supplier price before specifying it. The point of the valve is to use solar-heated water when the cylinder can supply it, and to avoid heating the combi from cold when it cannot.
