Electronic relay types

How the contact symbols on a relay or valve end-switch are drawn: poles, throws, normally open, normally closed, and break-before-make changeover. The hub KEY mixed NO and NC — the labels below follow the diagram.

On heating controls you meet these as a boiler or programmer relay, a room-stat changeover, or the end-switch on a motorised 2-port. The coil is drawn separately from the contacts. Normally means the contact position with the coil de-energised.

Contact arrangements

  • SPST (NO) — single pole, single throw, normally open. One circuit; closed only when the coil is energised.
  • SPST (NC) — single pole, single throw, normally closed. One circuit; opens when the coil is energised.
  • DPST (NO) — double pole, single throw, normally open. Two separate circuits switch together.
  • SPDT (B-M) — single pole, double throw, break-before-make. One common (C) leaves NC before it makes NO. This is a changeover contact.
  • DPDT (B-M) — double pole, double throw, break-before-make. Two changeovers ganged together.

The hub listed every type as “normally closed” and repeated SPST (NO). That does not match the drawing (NO, NC, DPST (NO), SPDT (B-M), DPDT (B-M)).

The animation is an SPDT changeover: with the coil off, the pole sits on NC. Energise the coil and it breaks NC, then makes NO.
