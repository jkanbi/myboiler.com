Vaillant ecoTEC exclusive 835 Green iQ

A Harrow combi swap from a Glow-worm 24cxi to a Vaillant ecoTEC exclusive 835 Green iQ, after a 3rd gen Nest went on first. The extra-condense heat exchanger and the kit around the boiler are the useful part of the note. The “A+ rated” line on the hub page needed a caveat.

The house already had a Glow-worm 24cxi combi. A 3rd generation Nest Learning Thermostat went in first. A few months later the old boiler was short of hot water and looked close to a breakdown. Three quotes later — two Vaillant options and one Intergas — the owners chose the ecoTEC exclusive 835 Green iQ. The family already knew Vaillant from a parent’s ecoTEC, which is a common and honest reason to pick a brand.

This is a project note from that install, not a current product brochure. Vaillant’s exclusive Green iQ combi range at the time was the 835 (35 kW DHW) and 843 (43 kW DHW). Later exclusive models use different badges (824 / 831 / 837 on Vaillant’s professional pages). Treat the figures below as the 835 as published, not a live price list.

Why the Green iQ, and what the claims meant

The extra efficiency on this boiler was Vaillant’s extra-condense stainless-steel heat exchanger: a second condensing stage that recovers more heat from the flue, especially in hot-water mode, and cuts the visible plume. That is a built-in recuperator, not a box you add on the flue later.

  • Modulation. Published heating range at 50/30 °C was about 3.9–26.4 kW, a ratio around 1:10. The hub’s “low modulation ratio” meant a wide turndown — the boiler can run at a small fraction of its maximum. That is the useful number for a small heat load.
  • Hot water. About 14.5 l/min at a 35 K rise on the 835. That is why it replaced a tired 24 kW-class Glow-worm that was no longer delivering.
  • ErP. The boiler itself was A for heating and A for hot water, with a published 94% ErP efficiency. The A+ heating band was not the boiler on its own. Vaillant’s own literature said A+ when a compatible control (they named vSMART at the time) was added. A Nest on switched live does not, by itself, make the label A+.
  • Controls and connectivity. The range was designed for Vaillant eBUS / app controls. A Nest can still run the heating — see Nest and a combi boiler — but it is not the path Vaillant used for that A+ package.

Green iQ mode on these boilers was Vaillant’s running-cycle optimisation and a recyclability claim (they published 85%). It is not a heat-pump hybrid and it does not remove the need to set a sensible flow temperature.

What went on the system

The boiler was not hung on dirty pipes. The job included a powerflush / system cleanse and inhibitor. That matters more than the badge on the case. The add-ons were:

  1. Spirotech Spirovent RV2 micro-bubble de-aerator on the flow. It takes dissolved air out of the circulating water. Less oxygen in the water is less corrosion. It does not replace inhibitor.
  2. Spirotech Spirotrap MB3 magnetic dirt filter on the return. Magnetic and non-magnetic particles. Spirotech’s 20-year guarantee on that filter is the figure they still publish; see the MB3 review.
  3. 15 mm inline magnetic scale device (Calmag on the original note) on the cold feed to the boiler. Treat that as a weak extra, not as a water softener. In hard-water London a softener, or at least an honest conversation about scale, is the stronger protection for a combi heat exchanger.
  4. Remote filling loop with a gauge (Altecnic on the original note). The 835 already had a built-in filling loop. The remote loop is a dosing point and a backup if the integral loop fails.

The hub page had Amazon product widgets on these parts. Those ads are stripped. Buy the current equivalent from a merchant the installer already uses, and keep the serials for any filter guarantee.

What this note is not

It is not an as-built record for a named address, and it is not a current “buy this boiler” recommendation. The 835 Green iQ is an older exclusive. If you are specifying a new Vaillant combi now, start from today’s exclusive / plus range, the live guarantee (Vaillant Advance and a boiler-protection kit were the extra years on the old exclusive), and whether you want eBUS weather compensation rather than an on/off Nest.

The alternative on the original quotes was Intergas. The 2-in-1 heat exchanger case for hard water is on the Intergas Combi Compact ECO RF review.
