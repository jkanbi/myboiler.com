Fixed-price boiler repairs

A manufacturer one-off repair is a capped visit with genuine parts. A local Gas Safe engineer is often cheaper for a single simple fault. The old hub figures (£299 Vaillant, £298 Worcester, £80 labour) are stale — use the official pages below.

Most boilers sit quietly until they do not. Then the choice is usually a local Gas Safe engineer you already know, a call-out network, or the manufacturer’s own out-of-guarantee repair. Several makers sell that last option as a fixed-price repair: one published fee for the visit, labour, and most boiler parts, rather than an open-ended parts-plus-time bill.

That is not the same as boiler cover (a monthly or annual plan) or an in-guarantee warranty claim. It is also not a substitute for the checks on our repairs page. Gas work in the UK still needs a Gas Safe registered engineer. If you can smell gas, hear a leak, or a carbon monoxide alarm is sounding, leave the property and call the National Gas Emergency Service on 0800 111 999.

What a manufacturer fixed-price repair is

You book the maker (or their appointed service arm), pay a published one-off fee, and their engineer attends with genuine parts. Typical extras on the official pages include a gas safety or combustion check, a short guarantee on the parts and labour they fit, and a reduced charge if the boiler cannot be repaired.

It is aimed at boilers that are out of manufacturer guarantee. If the appliance is still in warranty and the terms have been kept (annual service, Benchmark paperwork, registered installer), the repair should go through the guarantee, not a paid package. Say so when you book.

When the manufacturer cap makes sense

  • The engineer already knows you need more than one major part — for example a PCB and a fan — so a time-and-parts quote will sit near or above the published cap.
  • You want genuine OEM parts and the maker’s own short repair warranty (often 12 months on parts and 90 days on labour; Intergas states one year on parts and workmanship).
  • You would rather a factory-trained engineer who stocks that brand than a generalist who has to order the part.
  • The boiler is old enough to be out of guarantee, but not so far gone that the maker will call it beyond economical repair and only charge a diagnosis fee.

A local independent is still often the better buy for a single cheap part, a condensate freeze, a filling-loop or pressure top-up, or a fault that turns out not to be the boiler. Get a written quote from both routes before you pay the manufacturer fee — those fees are usually taken at booking.

Typical exclusions

Each brand writes its own list. Read the live page and the service schedule before you pay. Common themes on the official documents we checked in October 2026:

  • Vaillant — heat exchanger and expansion vessel are excluded from the fixed-price parts list. If they cannot repair it, they charge only the fixed engineer time (£99 on the current page).
  • Worcester Bosch — labour, VAT and most parts on the boiler and Worcester controls; a significant or structural part (they give shell, tank or back plate as examples), or a beyond-economical-repair decision, is re-quoted on the day. The 22 January 2026 service schedule also limits the visit to three major components and excludes heat banks, slave tanks and superstructure.
  • Baxi, Main and Potterton — one-off repair is call-out, parts, labour and VAT. If they cannot fix it they keep a £90 inspection fee and refund the rest. The published page does not list a heat-exchanger exclusion the way Vaillant does; confirm in the booking terms.
  • Intergas — labour and boiler parts on a fixed charge. If the fault is not the boiler they keep a call-out fee and refund the rest.
  • Glow-worm (Vaillant Group) — if it is uneconomical they charge £99 for engineer time. The public repair page does not publish a heat-exchanger exclusion.
  • Ideal — T&Cs treat beyond-economical-repair and similar no-repair outcomes as a partial refund, keeping the call-out and inspection element.

Scale, sludge, hidden pipework, flues, third-party controls, and the rest of the heating system are routinely outside a boiler-only package. That is why a manufacturer visit is not a system flush or a landlord safety certificate.

Independent quotes, without stale pound figures

The hub page used a 2017-ish parts table (pressure sensor about £20, fan £150, PCB £200, labour £80). Those numbers are not safe to republish. Trade part prices, VAT, and call-out rates move with the region and the brand. Treat the comparison below as a decision aid, dated October 2026, not a price list.

Kind of job Usually cheaper locally When the manufacturer cap can win
No parts, or a small sensor / PRV / condensate job Yes — one visit, cheap parts Rarely; the published cap is built for a bigger repair
One major part (fan, PCB, or pump) Often, if the engineer stocks or can source the part If the OEM part plus labour lands near the cap, or you want the maker’s repair warranty
Two or more major parts on the same visit Only if their written quote still undercuts the cap Usually — this is what the packages are for
Heat exchanger, expansion vessel, or a structural shell / tank Get both quotes; some maker packages exclude these Only if that brand’s live terms actually include the part

Ask any independent for a Gas Safe registration number, a written parts-and-labour figure, and whether the parts are genuine OEM. A cheaper pattern-part can look attractive and still leave you without the maker’s repair warranty.

VAT, booking, and “Repair & Care”

Vaillant, Worcester Bosch and Glow-worm state inc VAT on the consumer pages. Baxi says the one-off fee includes VAT. Ideal’s T&Cs say the £359 is inclusive of VAT. Intergas’s homeowner FAQ says their service appointments are inclusive of VAT; they do not print the repair figure. Viessmann’s table is silent — ask.

Several brands also sell a Repair & Care (or similar) plan through Domestic & General: you pay monthly for an initial repair plus a few months of cover, sometimes with a contribution if the boiler is written off. That is a different contract from the one-off fee. Do not mix the two prices.
